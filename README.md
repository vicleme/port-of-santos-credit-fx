# port-santos-credit-fx

**🇧🇷 [Leia isto em Português](README.pt-br.md)**

Analysis of the relationship between exchange rate volatility, bank credit, and cargo volume at the Port of Santos (2015–2025), using open data from Brazil's Central Bank (BCB) and port authority statistics. Data Science project — Fatec Baixada Santista.

## Topic

Relationship between exchange rate variation, bank credit, and cargo movement at the Port of Santos: an analysis of public data from the Central Bank and the Port Authority (2015–2025).

## General objective

To investigate, using public data, whether there is a statistical relationship between exchange rate variation, the volume of bank credit directed to foreign trade and logistics activities in the municipality of Santos, and cargo movement at the Port of Santos, over the period 2015–2025.

## Repository structure

```
data/
  raw/          -> raw data, as downloaded from the original sources
  processed/    -> monthly panel already cleaned and ready for analysis
```

## Processed data (`data/processed/`)

- **`painel_2015_2025.csv`** — main monthly panel, closed to the project's formal scope (2015–2025). 132 months, 13 columns. Use this one for the PI2 analysis.
- **`painel_2015_2026.csv`** — same panel, unclipped, including the extra 2026 months already downloaded for exchange rate and Comex Stat (ESTBAN is empty for those months). Kept as extra data for a possible future extension (e.g., a follow-up project).
- **`painel_modelagem_2015_2025.csv`** — the main panel plus `credito_ex_bradesco` (credit of the other banks with a branch in Santos, without Bradesco). The new column is filled only for 2015-01 to 2024-12 (120 months): 2025 has reporting gaps by bank. Generated by `src/build_modeling_panel.py`; used by notebooks 11 and 12.

## EDA roadmap and notebooks

The exploratory analysis follows a 6-step roadmap, documented in [`docs/02_roteiro_eda.md`](docs/02_roteiro_eda.md) (in Portuguese). The notebook number follows the **order of creation**, not the step number; the table below shows how they match.

| Notebook | Step | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| `01_eda_painel_2015_2025.ipynb` | 1. Univariate | Histogram/boxplot of each series; locates outliers. |
| `02_outlier_importacao_2021.ipynb` | Extension of 1 | Product-level (NCM) breakdown of the Sep-Oct/2021 import outlier. |
| `03_agencias_ativo_credito.ipynb` | Extension of 1 | Tests whether bank branch closures in Santos explain the pattern of `ativo_total`/`credito_total`. |
| `04_temporal_painel_2015_2025.ipynb` | 2. Temporal | Evolution of each series over time and seasonality by calendar month. |
| `05_tratamento_nans.ipynb` | 3. Missing values | Where the NaNs are (only ESTBAN columns, Oct-Dec/2025) and why they are dropped instead of interpolated. |
| `06_correlacao.ipynb` | 4. Correlation | Correlation matrix on levels (first indication, not proof). |
| `07_estacionariedade.ipynb` | 5. Stationarity | Augmented Dickey-Fuller test on each series, levels and first difference. |
| `08_decomposicao.ipynb` | 6. Decomposition | Trend, seasonality and residual of `cambio_venda` and `exportação_fob`. |
| `09_correlacao_diferenciada.ipynb` | Extension of 4 | Redoes the correlation on first differences, with significance tests and lagged (cross) correlation. |
| `10_estban_por_banco_degrau_2019.ipynb` | Modeling, part 1 | Opens ESTBAN by bank: the August/2019 step is concentrated in Bradesco. Zivot-Andrews test, comparison of four ways to use credit, and the modeling sample. |
| `11_log_estacionariedade_correlacao.ipynb` | Modeling, part 2 | ADF and correlation on the log series, now with credit without Bradesco (all four series are I(1)). |
| `12_cointegracao_granger.ipynb` | Modeling, part 3 | Engle-Granger and Johansen cointegration; Granger causality on a VAR of differenced logs, with Toda-Yamamoto as a robustness check. |

Open questions that the quantitative EDA cannot answer (for example, the cause of the August/2019 step, and why the exchange rate changed level in 2020) are listed in `docs/02_roteiro_eda.md`.

## Where the analysis stands

- Correlations between exchange rate, exports and imports are strong in levels and nearly vanish in first differences: much of what is seen in levels is shared trend (notebooks 06, 07, 09).
- The August/2019 step in `credito_total` is concentrated in one bank, with no change in branch count, which points to a reporting artifact. The cause is not publicly documented and was requested from the Central Bank (notebook 10). Credit without Bradesco is the credit series of the models.
- Cointegration is not robustly established, and Granger tests show no stable predictive precedence of the exchange rate or credit over cargo (notebooks 11 and 12). This is not proof of absence of a relationship, and Granger is prediction, not causality.
- Credit here is the credit of bank branches located in Santos, not credit to the Port or to foreign trade; Comex Stat covers maritime foreign trade, not all port movement.

The written work (article versions) is in [`docs/artigo/`](docs/artigo/).

## How to reproduce

```bash
cd src
python merge_datasets.py
```

Generates `painel_2015_2026.csv` and `painel_2015_2025.csv` in `data/processed/` from the raw files in `data/raw/`. Then:

```bash
python build_modeling_panel.py
```

Generates `painel_modelagem_2015_2025.csv` (the panel with credit without Bradesco), read by notebooks 11 and 12.

To run the notebooks, install the dependencies first:

```bash
pip install -r requirements.txt
```

Note: notebooks 01 to 09 read the data directly from this repository's raw GitHub URLs, so they need an internet connection. Notebooks 10 to 12 read from the local `data/` folder when it exists and fall back to the GitHub URLs otherwise.
